Decisions baked in

What this direction costs and what it buys you

Decision
Rationale
Risk
Light by default in a dark-by-default category
Every direct competitor (Linear, Height, Raycast, Cursor, Granola) defaults to dark mode. Going paper-light is an immediate "I'm different" signal that connects to the editorial / publication metaphor.
Indie hackers reflexively dark-mode every tool. They may bounce in the first 5 seconds. Mitigated by the ink-mode toggle and by making the choice feel intentional, not lazy.
Instrument Serif on ALL headings (not just AI output)
Pushes the editorial commitment all the way. Serifs say "this is a thought, not a UI label." Granola's exact bet, just turned up. The mood is consistent across marketing, app, and summary.
Reads slower than sans-serif. Could feel "less productive" to scan-skim users. Body stays in DM Sans so reading speed is preserved where it matters.
Brick red #A8341F as the accent
Masthead color. Saturated enough to be a CTA, warm enough to feel old-world. Pairs with paper and ink without going corporate-Salesforce.
More attention-grabbing than amber — danger of feeling "alarming" if used loosely. Same accent doubles as danger token to economize visual vocabulary.
Drop cap + italic pull quote on meeting summaries
The AI's output literally reads like a magazine article. Strongest signal that "this is the AI's voice, not the UI's."
If overused, becomes precious. Strictly scoped to: meeting summary lede, AI recommendation card title, marketing hero.
Newspaper rules (border-top: 4px ink) on major sections
Replaces shadowed cards as the elevation cue. Cheap, fast to render, instantly reads as "publication."
Looks "uncool" to anyone reading from a Glassmorphism 2.0 lens. That's the point.
Bottom-border-only form inputs
Document-like, à la Stripe Atlas / Read.cv onboarding. Lighter visually than boxed inputs. Focus expands to a 2px accent underline.
Less obvious where to click. Compensated by oversized clickable areas and clear labels above each field.
